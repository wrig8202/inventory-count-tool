01 / The problem

A daily count wasn’t
optional.

Material movements weren’t reliably reflected in the system. Planning depended on inventory, so I had to walk the floor and rebuild the picture every day.

01 — TIME

Count. Calculate.
Type it again.

The original workflow took roughly two hours: a paper walk, partial-container estimates, manual arithmetic, and spreadsheet entry.

02 — TRUST

A total without
a clear method.

Different counting approaches produced different answers. A number alone didn’t explain where the system and the floor disagreed.

03 — DECISIONS

Bad records become
bad plans.

Unrecorded usage, receipts, and moves distorted the starting point for supply decisions. Each discrepancy needed a location and a reason.

“Make the necessary count easier.
Make the resulting number useful.”The design goal

02 / The solution

Follow the work.
Remove the rework.

A familiar pizzeria makes the original engineering logic easy to experience. Three stops show how an observation becomes a planning input.

01 — WALK-IN COOLER

Verify the shelf.

Count mozzarella in the containers you can see. Convert to kilograms automatically and surface the shortage against the records.

02 — DRY STORAGE

Confirm what’s right.

The flour matches. Capture that confirmation, keep your place in the route, and move on without rewriting the same information.

03 — PREP STATION

Find the blind spot.

There’s sauce where the system shows none. Log the actual stock and leave a specific discrepancy to investigate.

03 / The result

Less time rebuilding.
More time deciding.

The tool brings the count, unit conversion, and discrepancy record into one workflow. The output is a structured inventory file and a focused list of issues to act on.

It makes the daily workaround more efficient and exposes recurring failures. Reliable scanning and transaction discipline still need to be fixed upstream.

The original write-up documents a roughly two-hour daily baseline. A measured after-time has not been supplied. The calculator is an illustration, not a claimed project result.
